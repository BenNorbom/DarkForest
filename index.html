Survival under pressure

Older production sites yield less supply. Garrison upkeep draws on the same stores that support the population. A viable refuge has maintained renewable local supply and a year of reserves for five years. A lineage can persist under foreign control.

Industrial and launch signatures propagate outward. Periodic deep-sky observations can expose a quiet system. A distant colony needs its own intelligence and reserve.

Trade and contested sites

Fast expeditions travel at one-third light speed. Survey craft inspect sites, settlement craft carry residents, and military expeditions commit most of their local garrison. Attackers need a 25% local strength advantage to take an occupied site.

Complementary goods earn more when local demand is fresh. Passage offers, successful exchanges, and prior conflicts influence future dealings. A trade return takes the full freight travel time.

Research and adaptation

Local research draws hidden breakthrough thresholds. Long-endurance drives extend practical reach, compact habitats reduce colony cost, interferometry improves passive sensing, and autonomous fabrication increases local supply.

Six pretrained policies compete for lineage survival. Each controller keeps its own history. A delivered success or failure changes its preference for that action and destination, and the effect fades as evidence ages.
